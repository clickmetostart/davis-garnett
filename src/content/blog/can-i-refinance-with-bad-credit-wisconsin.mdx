<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers a question we get often from homeowners who feel trapped by their current rate but worried their credit won't let them refinance.</em></p>

<h3>Can I refinance with bad credit in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  The short answer: it depends on what loan you currently have and what your goals are. Here's the honest breakdown:
</p>

<h3>FHA Streamline Refinance (Lowest Bar)</h3>
<p>
  If you currently have an FHA loan, the FHA Streamline is one of the most borrower-friendly refinance options available. It requires:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>No income verification in many cases</li>
  <li>No appraisal in most cases</li>
  <li>A history of on-time mortgage payments (typically 12 months)</li>
  <li>The refinance must provide a "net tangible benefit" (lower rate or payment)</li>
</ul>
<p>
  Credit score minimums for FHA Streamline start around <strong>580–620</strong> depending on the lender. We've done these with scores in the low 600s.
</p>

<h3>VA IRRRL (Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan)</h3>
<p>
  For veterans with existing VA loans, the IRRRL (streamline refi) has minimal credit requirements — similar to FHA Streamline. The focus is on payment history, not score.
</p>

<h3>Conventional Refinance</h3>
<p>
  Conventional refinancing typically requires a <strong>620 minimum</strong>, though the best rates don't kick in until 740+. If your score is below 620, conventional isn't available — but FHA or VA routes may still work.
</p>
