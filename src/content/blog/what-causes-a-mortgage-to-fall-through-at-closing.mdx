<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, gives Realtors an honest look at why deals collapse — and how to stop it from happening to yours.</em></p>

<h3>What causes a mortgage to fall through at closing?</h3>

<p>
  Every Realtor has had a deal fall apart. Here are the most common mortgage-side causes — and what to do about them:
</p>

<h3>1. Major Credit Change During Escrow</h3>
<p>
  Opening a new credit card, buying a car, or co-signing a loan between pre-approval and closing can tank a credit score or push DTI over the limit. I tell every buyer: <strong>do not touch your credit during escrow</strong>. Lenders pull credit again before closing.
</p>

<h3>2. Employment Change</h3>
<p>
  Job loss, quitting, or switching from W-2 to self-employed kills a loan instantly. We verify employment days before closing. Remind your clients: no job changes until after the keys are in their hands.
</p>

<h3>3. Appraisal Comes in Low</h3>
<p>
  If the home doesn't appraise at the purchase price and neither party is willing to renegotiate, the deal dies — unless the buyer has an appraisal contingency and chooses to walk.
</p>

<h3>4. Undisclosed Debt or Liabilities</h3>
<p>
  A lien discovered on title, a child support judgment, or an IRS garnishment showing up at underwriting can delay or kill a closing. Title search usually catches these — but not always upfront.
</p>

<h3>5. Wire Fraud / Closing Funds Issue</h3>
<p>
  Buyers who wire funds to the wrong account (due to phishing) or fail to wire on time can cause a same-day closing collapse. Always instruct buyers to call the title company directly — never trust wiring instructions received by email alone.
</p>

<h3>6. Lender Delay</h3>
<p>
  Some lenders simply can't execute. This is why I tell every agent partner: choose your lender as carefully as you choose your clients. We close on time, every time — or we make it right.
</p>
