<p><em>"What mortgage should I get?"</em> or <em>"What is the best first-time homebuyer loan?"</em> are questions we hear every single day.</p>

<p>The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) actively recommends that buyers compare Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, and state housing-finance programs based on their exact financial circumstances.</p>

<p>Because MadCity Home Loans is an independent broker, we aren't limited to pushing just one product from one bank. We have access to them all. Here is a master guide to help you decide which mortgage is right for you.</p>

<h3>1. Conventional Loans</h3>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Buyers with good-to-excellent credit (680+) and at least a 3% to 5% down payment.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The Pros:</strong> If you put down 20%, you avoid Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) entirely. Even if you put down less, the PMI on a conventional loan falls off automatically once you reach 20% equity.</li>
  <li><strong>The Cons:</strong> They have stricter credit and Debt-to-Income (DTI) requirements than government-backed loans.</li>
  <li><strong>First-Time Buyer Perks:</strong> Fannie Mae (HomeReady) and Freddie Mac (Home Possible) allow 3% down payments for qualifying buyers.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. FHA Loans</h3>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Buyers with lower credit scores (580+), limited cash, or higher debt-to-income ratios.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The Pros:</strong> Extremely flexible qualification standards. You can buy a home with just 3.5% down, and interest rates are often lower than Conventional rates.</li>
  <li><strong>The Cons:</strong> FHA loans require an Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP) and a monthly Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP) that lasts for the life of the loan (unless you refinance later or put 10% down initially).</li>
</ul>

<h3>3. VA Loans</h3>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Veterans, active-duty military, and eligible surviving spouses.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The Pros:</strong> This is the best mortgage on the market. <strong>0% down payment required, and absolutely NO monthly mortgage insurance (PMI).</strong> Rates are highly competitive, and credit requirements are flexible.</li>
  <li><strong>The Cons:</strong> You must have eligible military service. There is also a one-time VA Funding Fee (though it can be rolled into the loan or waived if you have a service-connected disability).</li>
</ul>

<h3>4. USDA Loans</h3>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Low-to-moderate-income buyers purchasing a home in a rural or suburban area.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The Pros:</strong> <strong>0% down payment required.</strong> USDA loans offer low interest rates and reduced mortgage insurance costs compared to FHA.</li>
  <li><strong>The Cons:</strong> Strict geographical restrictions. The home must be located in a USDA-eligible area (many towns outside of Madison, like parts of Waunakee or Oregon, qualify). You must also fall below the household income limits for your county.</li>
</ul>

<h3>5. WHEDA Loans (Wisconsin Only)</h3>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Wisconsin residents looking for down payment assistance.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The Pros:</strong> WHEDA (Wisconsin Housing and Economic Development Authority) offers below-market interest rates and loans specifically designed to cover your down payment and closing costs.</li>
  <li><strong>The Cons:</strong> Like USDA, WHEDA loans have household income limits and purchase price limits, though they are usually quite generous.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>💡 The Decision Tree:</strong><br />
Are you a Veteran? <strong>Get a VA Loan.</strong><br />
Are you buying rural with no down payment? <strong>Look at USDA.</strong><br />
Do you have great credit but need down payment help? <strong>Look at WHEDA.</strong><br />
Is your credit under 680? <strong>FHA is likely your best bet.</strong><br />
Do you have 20% to put down? <strong>Conventional is the clear winner.</strong></p>

<h3>Deep Dive Questions</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Can I switch loan types later?</strong> Yes! You can absolutely refinance from an FHA loan into a Conventional loan later down the road once your home goes up in value to drop your mortgage insurance.</li>
  <li><strong>Are FHA appraisals harder to pass?</strong> FHA and VA appraisers do look for basic health and safety issues (like peeling paint or broken handrails), but they are not the deal-killers many people claim they are.</li>
</ul>
