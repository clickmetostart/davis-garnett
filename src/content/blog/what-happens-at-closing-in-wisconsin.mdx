<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, walks buyers through closing day so nothing is a surprise.</em></p>

<h3>What happens at closing when buying a house in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  Closing day is exciting, but it can feel overwhelming if you don't know what to expect. Here's exactly how it plays out for our buyers in Wisconsin:
</p>

<h3>Who's at the Table?</h3>
<p>
  Typically: the buyer(s), the seller(s) (sometimes separately), a title company representative or closing attorney, and your Realtor. I'm usually available by phone if any last-minute questions come up on the mortgage side.
</p>

<h3>What You'll Sign</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Closing Disclosure (CD):</strong> Final breakdown of your loan terms, interest rate, monthly payment, and all closing costs. You should have received this 3 business days before closing.</li>
  <li><strong>Promissory Note:</strong> Your legal promise to repay the loan.</li>
  <li><strong>Deed of Trust / Mortgage:</strong> The document that gives the lender a lien on the property until the loan is paid off.</li>
  <li><strong>Various Title & Transfer Documents:</strong> Prepared by the title company to officially transfer ownership from seller to buyer.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What to Bring</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Government-issued photo ID</li>
  <li>Certified or cashier's check for your down payment and closing costs (or wire confirmation if wired in advance)</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Long Does It Take?</h3>
<p>
  Plan for <strong>60–90 minutes</strong> for a typical purchase closing. Cash deals are faster; purchase loans with lots of disclosure pages can run a little longer.
</p>
<p>
  By the end, you'll have keys in hand and officially be a Wisconsin homeowner.
</p>
