<h3>What is title insurance and do I need it in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  Title insurance protects against problems with a property's ownership history — things like undiscovered liens, unpaid taxes from a prior owner, fraud, clerical errors, or disputes over ownership that can surface after you've already purchased the home.
</p>

<h3>Two Types of Title Insurance</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Lender's Title Insurance:</strong> Required on virtually every mortgage. It protects the lender's financial interest in the property. The buyer pays for this at closing — it's a standard closing cost.</li>
  <li><strong>Owner's Title Insurance:</strong> Optional but strongly recommended. It protects <em>your</em> equity and ownership rights. It's typically a one-time premium paid at closing and covers you for as long as you own the property.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Does It Actually Protect Against?</h3>
<p>
  Real examples I've seen in Wisconsin: a prior owner's contractor placed a mechanic's lien on the property that wasn't discovered until after purchase. A forged deed from decades ago that surfaced during a chain-of-title search. An heir claiming an interest in a property sold by an estate without proper probate.
</p>
<p>
  None of these are common — but they do happen, and when they do, the cost to resolve them can be enormous. Owner's title insurance is typically $300–$600 for a $350,000 property. It's cheap for what it covers.
</p>
