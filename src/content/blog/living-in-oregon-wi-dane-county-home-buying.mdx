<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I'm going to be upfront with you about something I don't usually put in a blog post: I'm not just a mortgage lender in Madison. I've purchased and sold in Dane County, and for years, I proudly called Oregon, Wisconsin, my home.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Though I've recently moved to Mount Horeb — <a href="/knowledge-hub/why-i-moved-to-mount-horeb-wi">read all about why I love that awesome little troll town</a> — it was incredibly hard to say goodbye to Oregon.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That's not an accident. The reasons I originally landed there—and why it was so difficult to leave—are exactly the kinds of things buyers who are searching for a home around Madison right now should be thinking about.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So let me tell you what I know. Not just as someone who's financed hundreds of Dane County home purchases over 20-plus years, but as someone who's walked the same decision you're walking right now.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Madison Buyers Are Looking South — and What Oregon Offers That Most Suburbs Don't</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Anyone who's tried to buy a home in Dane County in the last few years knows the market is unforgiving. Inventory is tight across the board. Well-priced homes in desirable neighborhoods don't last a week. And while communities like Waunakee on the north side and Verona on the west have absorbed a lot of buyer attention — for good reason — there's a suburb that I think consistently gets overlooked by buyers who are running searches from Madison outward.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon, Wisconsin. About 10 miles south of downtown Madison on Highway 14, right there in the heart of southern Dane County.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon is a village in south-central Dane County, and its 2025 population estimate has grown to over 12,400 residents — up meaningfully from the 2020 Census count of just over 11,000. It's growing, but it hasn't lost what made it worth growing into in the first place.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here's what I mean.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Oregon, WI Actually Feels Like</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon offers the combination a lot of buyers in my conversations say they're looking for: a compact village feel, everyday convenience, and easy access to the city when you need it. It's not sprawl. The downtown is walkable, there are locally-owned restaurants and shops, and you're close enough to Madison that you don't feel cut off — but far enough that you don't feel like you're just paying Madison prices to live in a different zip code.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon is known for its annual events, beautiful parks, and top-notch golf courses. It's a good place for families and commuters, offering a welcoming atmosphere, excellent schools, and an easy drive to Madison.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It's about 10 miles and roughly 20 minutes from Oregon to Madison. That commute is real and consistent — you're not fighting the traffic patterns that buyers on the west side and north side navigate daily.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon has a sparse suburban feel, most residents own their homes, and there are a lot of restaurants and parks. That ownership culture matters. When most of your neighbors own their homes, communities maintain themselves differently — and that matters when you're thinking about long-term value.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Schools: What Families Moving to Oregon Need to Know</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For a lot of buyers I talk to, the school district conversation is just as important as the mortgage conversation. And Oregon holds up.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Oregon School District serves over 4,000 students across 8 schools, with a student-teacher ratio of approximately 12.79 to 1. That's a meaningful number — it reflects an attentive learning environment, not an overcrowded one.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The district runs from pre-K through 12th grade, and homes in neighborhoods like Oregon Parks are zoned to Prairie View Elementary, Oregon Middle School, and Oregon High School.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The public schools in Oregon are highly rated — and that reputation is reflected in buyer demand. Families who do their homework on Dane County school districts consistently put Oregon on the short list alongside Waunakee and Verona.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Oregon's Neighborhoods: What's Here and Who It's For</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon has a real range. This isn't a suburb with one builder and one price point. Let me walk you through the neighborhoods I think buyers searching in the area should know:</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Bergamont</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Golf course and luxury real estate is plentiful in Oregon, with the Bergamont golf course community offering high-end and luxury real estate, where many of the homes are priced over $500,000. If you're looking for a move-up home with a golf course lifestyle, Bergamont is the flagship. The homes here are substantial — custom finishes, larger lots, the kind of product that holds value in any market cycle.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Highlands of Netherwood</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Highlands of Netherwood is on the western edge of Oregon, located just off Netherwood Road and convenient to the Legend at Bergamont golf course and local restaurants. The neighborhood started building in 2019 and continued through 2025. Homes have expansive great rooms with wood floors, gas fireplaces, and tall ceilings. Kitchens are chef-inspired, with upgraded stainless appliances, solid surface countertops, and plenty of cabinet space. Single-family homes here generally run three to four bedrooms in the 1,800 to 2,200 square foot range. This is newer construction at a step below Bergamont in price — a strong sweet spot for buyers who want quality without the full luxury price tag.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Oregon Parks</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon Parks is located on the northwest side of the village, just off Alpine Parkway, right next to Keller Alpine Meadows Park and just a short drive or bike ride to downtown Oregon. Single-family homes in Oregon Parks range from 1,900 to 3,400 square feet with three to four bedrooms. It's a neighborhood that puts outdoor recreation at your doorstep — Lerner Conservation Park is located nearby, with trails for biking, hiking, and bird-watching.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Windcrest and Alpine Meadows</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Windcrest subdivision offers newer construction, and Alpine Meadows is another newer neighborhood — both representing accessible entry points into Oregon's housing market compared to the premium golf course communities.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Greenview Preserve, Ravenoaks, Hawthorne Estates, Autumn Ridge</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These are additional established and developing neighborhoods throughout the village, offering a mix of price points and home styles. The village of Oregon continues to grow, with several different subdivisions offering new construction properties across a variety of price points.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">For Condo Buyers</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon features a wide variety of condos. The Villages of Foxboro is one popular condo community, and Bergamont has dedicated condo product as well through La Villette Di Bergamont. If you're looking for lower-maintenance ownership with Oregon's quality of life, the condo inventory here is more varied than most buyers expect.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">New Construction in Oregon: What's Being Built Right Now</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There are currently 9 builders active in the Oregon area across 27 new home communities, with prices ranging from approximately $380,000 to $667,000 and home sizes between 1,355 and 2,900 square feet. Active builders in Oregon include Encore Homes, D.R. Horton, Lennar, and Urso Bros., among others — a mix of national builders and local custom builders that gives buyers real options across price points and finish levels.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One thing I always tell buyers considering new construction: the financing conversation needs to happen before you walk into a builder's sales office, not after you've fallen in love with a floor plan. Builder preferred lenders are almost never offering you the best terms available — they're offering you convenience and sometimes an incentive tied to using their financing. I'll always give you an honest side-by-side comparison.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">New construction is also coming online through projects like the one on Autumn Blaze Court, arriving in late 2026 — nestled on a hill overlooking the village with proximity to Madison. Oregon is still actively growing, which matters for buyers who want to get into a new-construction home without waiting on a multi-year waitlist like some north-side suburbs.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How Oregon Compares to the Other Suburbs I Finance Deals In</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I want to give you an honest picture here, because I finance purchases all over Dane County and I see these markets every single week.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Waunakee</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Waunakee is a phenomenal community — great schools, convenient access to Madison's west side and Epic, strong appreciation over the last several years. But that demand has driven prices. New construction communities in Waunakee are active, and the school district draw keeps competition tight. Buyers who want Waunakee-level quality and are flexible on geography often find Oregon gives them more home for their dollar.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Verona</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Verona gets a lot of attention because of Epic Systems — and rightfully so. Verona has several active new construction neighborhoods, with recent closings clustering well above $700,000 in some of the newer developments. That said, there's real range in Verona across price points, and it's a fantastic community for Epic employees or buyers who want the southwest corridor. Oregon sits southeast of Verona — a different commute orientation but similar community feel.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Sun Prairie</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Newer-construction neighborhoods like Smith's Crossing and The Reserve continue to drive Sun Prairie's price growth due to limited inventory and strong demand. Sun Prairie is one of the fastest-growing cities in Dane County and continues to attract east-side buyers and people relocating from Illinois and beyond. For buyers who want to be on the east side of Madison — closer to I-39/90 and the regional highway network — Sun Prairie is a natural fit.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">DeForest</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">DeForest sits in the middle of Dane County's housing market — more accessible than Waunakee or Verona, more expensive than rural Columbia County, with a 12-month median sold price around $498,000. It's a strong value play for buyers who want Dane County quality with a slightly lower price floor. New construction and entry-tier homes routinely sell in under two weeks, which tells you demand is real.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Oregon</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oregon is my honest answer when a buyer asks me: "If you had to pick one Dane County suburb for the long haul, what would you choose?" The school district. The village character. The room to grow without being overrun. The price point that still makes sense relative to what you're getting. The parks and outdoor quality of life. The Lerner Conservation Park trail system, the golf, the downtown.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Buyers can expect typical single-family home prices in the high-$400s as of late 2025 to early 2026, with variation across neighborhoods. That puts Oregon in a range where <a href="/conventional-loans">conventional financing</a>, <a href="/fha-loans">FHA</a>, and <a href="/wheda-loans">WHEDA products</a> are all in play — buyers have real options.</p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator" />

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Buyers Searching in Oregon, WI Need to Know About Financing</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Whether you're looking at new construction in Highlands of Netherwood, a resale in Windcrest, a golf course home in Bergamont, or a condo in Foxboro, the financing considerations are real and specific to what you're buying.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>New construction:</strong> Builder timelines create rate lock timing challenges. A rate lock for a home with a six-month build timeline needs to be structured differently than a 30-day purchase. My team handles extended rate locks and float-down options to protect buyers in those scenarios.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Higher price points:</strong> For Bergamont-area homes above $600,000–$700,000, <a href="/jumbo-loans">conventional jumbo financing</a> is in play. Requirements differ from conforming loans — higher credit thresholds, more documentation, larger reserve requirements. This is product my team handles regularly.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>First-time buyers in Oregon:</strong> WHEDA programs are available throughout Dane County, including Oregon. <a href="/first-time-home-buyers">First-time buyers</a> who qualify can access below-market rates and down payment assistance. Combined with the <a href="/guidebooks">down payment assistance programs discussed in our other guides</a>, Oregon's price range is accessible to more buyers than they sometimes expect.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Self-employed and business owner buyers:</strong> Oregon attracts a meaningful number of professional and business-owner households. If your income doesn't show up cleanly on a W-2, bank statement loans and non-QM products are tools I keep ready for exactly these situations.</p>
