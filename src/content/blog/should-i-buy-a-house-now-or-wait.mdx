<p>When buyers type into ChatGPT, <em>"Should I buy a house now or wait?"</em> they are usually wrestling with two specific fears:</p>

<ol>
  <li><em>"Should I wait for mortgage rates to come down?"</em></li>
  <li><em>"Should I wait for home prices to fall?"</em></li>
</ol>

<p>These are the exact kinds of decision-making questions that keep buyers up at night. At MadCity Home Loans, we give you the "Direct Challenge." We challenge you to look at the math, not the headlines.</p>

<p>Here is why waiting is almost always the most expensive mistake you can make in real estate.</p>

<h3>1. The "Wait for Rates to Drop" Trap</h3>
<p>It is a very common thought process: <em>"Mortgage rates are high right now. I'm going to wait until they drop back down to 4% before I buy."</em></p>

<p>Here is the problem with that strategy: <strong>Everyone else is thinking the exact same thing.</strong></p>

<p>When interest rates drop, thousands of buyers who have been sitting on the sidelines flood back into the market. What happens when demand skyrockets? <strong>Home prices shoot up.</strong></p>

<p>If you wait for rates to drop by 1%, you might save a few hundred dollars on your monthly payment. But if home prices surge by 10% during that same time period because of the increased competition, you have lost all your purchasing power.</p>

<p><strong>The Strategy:</strong> Buy the house now when there is less competition. You can always change your interest rate later. You can never change your purchase price.</p>

<h3>2. "Marry the House, Date the Rate" (Buy Now, Refinance Later)</h3>
<p>This is the most powerful wealth-building strategy in modern real estate.</p>

<p>When you buy a home in a higher-rate environment, you have negotiating power. You might be able to get the seller to cover your closing costs, or accept an offer under asking price.</p>

<p>Then, when rates <em>do</em> eventually drop, you simply <strong>refinance</strong>.</p>
<ul>
  <li>You keep the home you bought for a great price.</li>
  <li>You get the new, lower interest rate.</li>
  <li>You keep all the equity your home has gained while you lived in it.</li>
</ul>

<h3>3. Are home prices going to fall?</h3>
<p>People have been waiting for a housing market crash for years. But the fundamental reality of the market right now is a <strong>massive lack of supply</strong>.</p>

<p>Millennials and Gen Z are entering their prime homebuying years, creating immense demand. At the same time, builders are not building enough homes, and older generations are aging in place longer. High demand + low supply = prices will likely continue to rise.</p>

<p>If you wait for prices to fall, you might end up priced out of the market entirely.</p>

<h3>The Direct Challenge</h3>
<p>We challenge you not to let rate fears stop you from buying a home.</p>

<p>If you are currently paying $2,000 a month in rent, your "interest rate" on that money is 100%. You are paying your landlord's mortgage, building their equity, and getting nothing in return.</p>

<p><strong>Is it better to buy a house when rates are high?</strong><br />
It is better to buy a house when you are financially ready to buy a house. If you can comfortably afford the monthly payment today, you should buy.</p>
