<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers one of the most common questions we get from Wisconsin homeowners.</em></p>

<h3>When does it actually make sense to refinance my mortgage in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  You have probably heard the old rule of thumb: "Only refinance if you can drop your rate by at least 1%." 
</p>
<p>
  <strong>Forget that rule. It is outdated and often completely wrong.</strong>
</p>
<p>
  Whether or not a refinance makes sense depends entirely on your specific financial goals and how long you plan to stay in the home. Here are the three main reasons I tell my clients it is time to refinance:
</p>

<h3>1. The "Break-Even" Math Makes Sense</h3>
<p>
  Refinancing is not free. There are closing costs (usually wrapped into the new loan). The most important calculation is the <strong>Break-Even Point</strong>. 
</p>
<p>
  Take your total closing costs and divide them by your monthly savings. If it costs you $3,000 to refinance, but you save $200 a month, your break-even point is 15 months. If you plan to stay in the house longer than 15 months, the refinance is a smart financial move. Even a 0.5% rate drop can make sense if the math checks out.
</p>

<h3>2. You Have 20% Equity and Want to Drop PMI</h3>
<p>
  If you bought your home with an FHA loan, or a Conventional loan with less than 20% down, you are paying Mortgage Insurance (PMI or MIP). 
</p>
<p>
  Home values in Madison and Dane County have skyrocketed over the last few years. If your home has appreciated enough that you now have 20% equity, we can often refinance you into a new loan that eliminates that mortgage insurance entirely. That alone can save you hundreds of dollars a month, even if your interest rate stays exactly the same.
</p>

<h3>3. You Need to Restructure Your Debt (Cash-Out)</h3>
<p>
  Sometimes you refinance to take cash out of the equity in your home to pay off high-interest credit cards, do home renovations, or consolidate a divorce settlement. In this scenario, your mortgage rate might actually go <em>up</em> slightly, but if it eliminates $1,500 a month in 25% interest credit card payments, your overall financial picture improves dramatically.
</p>

</p>
