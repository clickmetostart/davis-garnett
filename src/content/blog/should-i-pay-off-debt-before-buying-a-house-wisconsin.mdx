<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, gives Wisconsin buyers the honest math on debt vs. down payment tradeoffs.</em></p>

<h3>Should I pay off debt before buying a house?</h3>

<p>
  This is one of the most nuanced questions in personal finance, and the answer is: <em>it depends on which debt and what it does to your qualification.</em> Here's how I think through it with clients:
</p>

<h3>Pay Off Debt If It's Killing Your DTI</h3>
<p>
  If your Debt-to-Income ratio is above the threshold for the loan you want, paying off specific debts can push you into qualifying range. The best targets are:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>High monthly payment, low balance:</strong> A car loan with $4,000 remaining but a $425/month payment is costing you a lot of DTI per dollar. Paying it off drops your DTI significantly.</li>
  <li><strong>Credit cards:</strong> Minimum payments on large credit card balances count against your DTI. Paying them down also improves your credit score.</li>
  <li><strong>Short-term debt:</strong> Accounts with fewer than 10 months remaining can sometimes be excluded from DTI calculation — ask us before you pay anything off.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Don't Pay Off Debt If It Drains Your Down Payment or Reserves</h3>
<p>
  Using your last $15,000 to pay off a car loan and then buying a home with nothing in reserves is often the wrong move. Lenders want to see reserves after closing. And a larger down payment reduces your PMI and monthly payment more than eliminating one debt account.
</p>

</p>
