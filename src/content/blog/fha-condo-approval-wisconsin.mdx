<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers one of the most common qualification questions we get from Wisconsin buyers.</em></p>

<h3>Are condos in Madison hard to buy with an FHA loan?</h3>

<p>
  If you are looking at buying a condo in the Madison area using an FHA loan, I need to be upfront with you: it can be a frustrating process if you don't have a lender who knows exactly what they are doing. 
</p>
<p>
  The short answer is <strong>yes, it is more difficult than buying a single-family home</strong>, but it is absolutely possible.
</p>

<h3>The FHA Condo Approval Problem</h3>
<p>
  When you buy a single-family home with an FHA loan, the FHA only cares about the house you are buying. But when you buy a condo, you are buying into a shared building. The FHA views this as higher risk, so they require the <strong>entire condo association</strong> to be "FHA Approved."
</p>
<p>
  This means the condo association must meet strict financial and structural guidelines, such as:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>At least 50% of the units must be owner-occupied (not rentals).</li>
  <li>No more than 15% of the units can be behind on their HOA dues.</li>
  <li>The HOA must have adequate reserve funds set aside for major repairs (like a new roof).</li>
  <li>Commercial space in the building cannot exceed 35% of the total floor area.</li>
</ul>

<h3>The Reality in Madison</h3>
<p>
  Here is the catch: many condo associations in Madison and Dane County simply do not bother to keep their FHA approval active because the paperwork is tedious for the property managers. 
</p>
<p>
  If you fall in love with a condo, write an offer, and then your lender discovers the building isn't FHA approved, your deal will fall apart. 
</p>

<h3>How We Solve This at MadCity Home Loans</h3>
<p>
  We attack this problem from two angles:
</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Single-Unit Approvals (SUA):</strong> If a building isn't on the master FHA approved list, we can often request a "Single-Unit Approval" just for the unit you want to buy. This takes extra work from our underwriting team, but we do it all the time to save deals.</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Alternatives:</strong> Often, the best solution is to pivot. We offer Conventional loans that require as little as 3% down (which is actually less than FHA's 3.5%). Conventional condo rules are significantly less strict than FHA rules, and this often bypasses the entire headache.</li>
</ol>
