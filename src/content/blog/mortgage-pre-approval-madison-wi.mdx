<h2>Pre-Qualification vs. Pre-Approval: Why the Difference Matters</h2>

<p>These terms get used interchangeably online, but they are not the same thing — and in Madison's market, the distinction can cost you a home.</p>

<p><strong>Pre-qualification</strong> is an informal estimate. A lender asks you to describe your income, assets, and debts — no documentation required, no hard credit pull in most cases. They run some quick math and tell you approximately what you might qualify for. Pre-qualification letters are easy to get in minutes. They're also close to meaningless in a competitive offer situation, because a listing agent has no way to verify any of it.</p>

<p><strong>Pre-approval</strong> means a lender has actually done the work:
- Pulled your credit (hard pull, with real scores from all three bureaus)
- Reviewed income documentation — pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, or other income verification depending on loan type
- Reviewed asset documentation — bank statements, retirement accounts, gift letter documentation
- Run the file through automated underwriting (DU or LP) and received an approval finding
- Identified any conditions that need to be cleared before final approval</p>

<p>A pre-approval letter backed by this documentation is a genuine credit commitment. It tells a seller: this buyer has been checked out. The financing is real.</p>

<h2>The Full Pre-Approval Checklist for Madison Buyers</h2>

<p>To issue a proper pre-approval, Rob Miller's team will need the following documentation (the exact list varies by loan type and employment situation):</p>

<p><strong>Income:</strong>
- Most recent 30 days of pay stubs (or equivalent)
- W-2s from the past two years
- Federal tax returns from the past two years (especially for self-employed buyers — see below)
- Most recent employer contact information</p>

<p><strong>Assets:</strong>
- Two most recent months of bank statements for all accounts being used for down payment and closing costs
- Retirement account statements (401k, IRA) if applicable
- Gift letter and documentation if receiving a down payment gift</p>

<p><strong>Credit:</strong>
- Authorization to pull credit — this is done by the lender
- If there are known credit issues (past late payments, collections, or a prior short sale or foreclosure), having those details ready up front allows for honest conversation about timelines and options</p>

<p><strong>Property:</strong>
- At the pre-approval stage, you don't need a specific property — the approval is based on your financial profile. Once you have a property under contract, it gets evaluated separately through appraisal and title.</p>

<h2>Special Considerations for Self-Employed Madison Buyers</h2>

<p>Self-employed buyers, freelancers, business owners, and 1099 earners face additional documentation requirements because their income is verified differently than a salaried employee's.</p>

<p>For conventional and government-backed loans, lenders typically require:
- Two years of federal tax returns (business and personal)
- A year-to-date profit and loss statement
- Verification that the business is active (often through a CPA letter or business license)</p>

<p>Here's the issue: many self-employed Madison buyers legally minimize their taxable income through deductions. What the IRS sees and what the buyer actually earns can be very different numbers. Traditional underwriting uses the tax return figure — which can dramatically understate the buyer's real financial capacity.</p>

<p>In cases where tax returns understate income, Rob's team looks at alternative documentation products:
- <strong>Bank statement loans:</strong> Qualify based on 12 or 24 months of business or personal bank deposits, not tax returns
- <strong>Asset depletion / asset-based underwriting:</strong> Use liquid asset reserves to derive qualifying income
- <strong>P&L loans:</strong> Qualify based on a CPA-prepared profit and loss statement, bypassing traditional tax return review</p>

<p>Madison has a significant self-employed population — from downtown professionals to Middleton-based tech business owners — and the standard qualification model doesn't always serve them. Rob's team knows the alternatives.</p>

<h2>What a Pre-Approval Does — and Doesn't — Lock In</h2>

<p>A pre-approval is a credit commitment based on the information reviewed at the time it's issued. It is not a rate lock and it is not a guarantee of final approval. Here's what changes between pre-approval and clear to close:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>The property:</strong> Once you have a contract, the property gets appraised and reviewed separately</li>
  <li><strong>Your financial situation:</strong> If anything changes after pre-approval — new debt, a job change, a large asset movement — it needs to be disclosed. These changes can affect the final loan.</li>
  <li><strong>Interest rate:</strong> You don't lock your rate until you have a property under contract and you've discussed lock timing with Rob's team</li>
</ul>

<p>The pre-approval stage is also when any conditions that need to be resolved — credit issues, documentation gaps, program eligibility questions — get identified. Better to find and resolve them now than during the transaction.</p>
