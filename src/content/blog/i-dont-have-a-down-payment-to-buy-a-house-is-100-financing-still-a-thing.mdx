<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One of the most common things I hear from potential homebuyers  especially first-time buyers is:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“I’d love to buy a home, but I don’t have a down payment.”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Totally understandable. Saving up tens of thousands of dollars while also dealing with rising rent, everyday bills, and just… life, isn’t exactly easy.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So, the big question is  <br>Is 100% financing still a thing?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The answer: Yes. It still exists. And it’s more accessible than most people think.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let me break it down for you.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The 100% Financing Option Most Buyers Don’t Know About</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A client came to me recently, feeling a little discouraged.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They had a solid job, great credit, and were preapproved for a mortgage   but the one thing holding them back was the down payment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They asked, “Isn’t there any way to buy a home without putting 5%, 10%, or even 20% down?”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s when I introduced them to a <strong>100% financing option</strong> that splits the loan into <strong>two parts</strong>   no down payment required.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let’s walk through a real example using a $400,000 home:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>First mortgage (97%)</strong> = $388,000</li>



<li><strong>Second mortgage (3%)</strong> = $12,000</li>



<li>That adds up to <strong>100% financing</strong>   the entire purchase price covered.</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You read that right: no down payment needed.</p>



<h5 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Monthly Payment Breakdown:</strong></h5>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Naturally, the next question was:<br><strong>“Okay, so what would my monthly payment look like?”</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here’s the breakdown for the&nbsp;<strong>first mortgage</strong>:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Principal &amp; Interest</strong>: $2,581</li>



<li><strong>Estimated Taxes</strong>: $500</li>



<li><strong>Home Insurance</strong>: $300</li>



<li><strong>Mortgage Insurance</strong>: $325</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That brings the&nbsp;<strong>total monthly payment</strong>&nbsp;to about&nbsp;<strong>$3,706.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Now, here’s the best part:</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <strong>second loan</strong>   the one covering the last 3% of the purchase   comes with:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>✅ 0% interest</strong><br><strong>✅ $0 monthly payment</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s right. You’re not required to make payments on that second portion, and it doesn’t accrue interest.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This setup makes homeownership possible for buyers who are financially stable but just haven’t had the chance to save up for a large down payment.</p>



<h5 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What You Do Need to Cover</strong></h5>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While you don’t need a down payment with this structure, you do still need to account for closing costs.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Closing costs typically range from 2% to 4% of the home’s purchase price. In this example, that would be somewhere between&nbsp;<strong>$8,000–$16,000.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But here’s a strategy:<br>Many buyers negotiate a <strong>seller credit</strong> to help cover these expenses   especially in markets where homes are sitting a little longer and sellers are more flexible.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So yes, it’s entirely possible to walk into a home with little to no money out of pocket.</p>



<h5 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is 100% Financing Right for You?</strong></h5>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Like any loan product, this isn’t a one-size-fits-all solution.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s a great option for buyers who have strong income and credit but don’t have liquid cash for a down payment. That said, your eligibility will depend on a few factors, like:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Income and debt-to-income ratio</li>



<li>Credit score</li>



<li>Property location (some programs are specific to certain areas)</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s where working with a mortgage expert (👋 hi, that’s me) can make a big difference. I’ll walk you through your options, compare loan types, and make sure you understand the pros and cons of each one.</p>



<h5 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Final Thoughts</strong></h5>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you’ve been holding off on homeownership because of the down payment   it might be time to take a second look.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yes, 100% financing is real. Yes, it’s available in 2025. And yes, with the right guidance, it could be your ticket to finally buying a home of your own.</p>
