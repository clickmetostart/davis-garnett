<h3>What is a bridge loan and do I need one in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  A bridge loan is a short-term loan (typically 6–12 months) that allows you to borrow against the equity in your current home to fund the down payment or purchase of a new home — <em>before</em> your current home sells.
</p>

<h3>How It Works</h3>
<ol>
  <li>You apply for a bridge loan secured by your current home's equity</li>
  <li>You use those funds as the down payment on your new home</li>
  <li>You close on the new home and move in</li>
  <li>Your old home sells, you pay off the bridge loan with the proceeds</li>
</ol>
<p>
  During the bridge period, you may be paying a mortgage on the old home, a bridge loan payment, and a new mortgage on the new home — though some bridge loans are structured as interest-only to minimize the carrying cost.
</p>

<h3>When Does a Bridge Loan Make Sense?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>You've found your dream home and can't make a contingent offer competitive enough</li>
  <li>You have substantial equity in your current home</li>
  <li>You're confident your current home will sell quickly (strong market, priced right)</li>
  <li>The carrying cost is manageable for your income</li>
</ul>

<h3>When to Think Twice</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Your current home is in a slow-selling market or overpriced</li>
  <li>Bridge loan interest rates (typically prime + 1–2%) would strain your budget</li>
  <li>You could negotiate a rent-back or find temporary housing instead</li>
</ul>
