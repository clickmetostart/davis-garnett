<h3>How do I remove PMI from my mortgage in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) was added to your loan because you put less than 20% down on a conventional mortgage. The good news: it doesn't last forever, and there are ways to get rid of it faster than you might think.
</p>

<h3>Method 1: Automatic Cancellation (Federal Law — Homeowners Protection Act)</h3>
<p>
  By law, PMI must be automatically cancelled when your loan balance reaches <strong>78% of the original purchase price</strong> — based on your original amortization schedule. You don't have to do anything; the lender is required to cancel it.
</p>

<h3>Method 2: Request Cancellation at 80% LTV</h3>
<p>
  You don't have to wait until 78%. Once your loan balance drops to <strong>80% of the original purchase price</strong>, you can formally request cancellation. You typically need:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>A good payment history (no 30-day lates in the past 12 months)</li>
  <li>Written request to your loan servicer</li>
  <li>Sometimes a new appraisal (check with your servicer)</li>
</ul>

<h3>Method 3: New Appraisal Based on Current Value</h3>
<p>
  If your home has appreciated significantly, you may have reached 20% equity sooner than the amortization schedule shows. Some lenders will order a new appraisal and cancel PMI based on the current market value, not the original purchase price. This is lender-dependent and usually requires 2+ years of ownership.
</p>

<h3>Method 4: Refinance</h3>
<p>
  If you have 20%+ equity based on current value, refinancing into a new conventional loan eliminates PMI entirely. If current rates are favorable, this can be a double win — lower rate and no PMI.
</p>

<p>
  <strong>Note:</strong> FHA MIP works differently and generally cannot be cancelled without refinancing into a conventional loan. If you're on an FHA loan with growing equity, call us — a conventional refi may save you $200+/month.
</p>
