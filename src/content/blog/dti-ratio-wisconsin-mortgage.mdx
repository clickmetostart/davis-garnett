<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers one of the most common qualification questions we get from Wisconsin buyers.</em></p>

<h3>What is the maximum DTI (Debt-to-Income) ratio allowed in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  When I sit down with buyers in Madison, one of their biggest worries is their existing debt. "Do I have too many student loans?" "Will my car payment stop me from buying a house?" 
</p>
<p>
  The answer depends on your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio. Your DTI is simply your total monthly debt payments (including your future mortgage) divided by your gross monthly income.
</p>
<p>
  Here is the reality of what we can actually approve in Wisconsin right now:
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>FHA Loans:</strong> FHA is the most forgiving. We can often push your total DTI all the way up to <strong>56.9%</strong> if you have strong compensating factors (like a decent credit score or reserves).</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans (Fannie/Freddie):</strong> Typically, the absolute cap here is <strong>50%</strong>. However, to get the best rates and easiest automated underwriting approval, keeping it under 45% is ideal.</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loans:</strong> The VA technically does not have a hard DTI cap; they focus more on "residual income" (how much cash you have left over at the end of the month). That said, once you cross <strong>41%</strong>, we have to meet stricter residual income requirements. I've successfully closed VA loans with DTIs well over 50%.</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loans:</strong> These are the strictest. USDA typically caps your "front-end" (housing only) DTI at 29% and your "back-end" (total debt) DTI at <strong>41%</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<h3>The "Real Life" DTI vs. The "Bank" DTI</h3>
<p>
  A crucial piece of advice I give my clients: just because we <em>can</em> approve you at a 50% DTI doesn't mean you <em>should</em> max it out. The bank doesn't know how much you spend on groceries, daycare, or traveling. We will always run the numbers to show you the absolute maximum you qualify for, but then we work backward to find a monthly payment you are actually comfortable sleeping at night with.
</p>
<p>
  Also, keep in mind that not all debt counts against you the same way. For example, if you have a car loan with only 9 months of payments left, we can often exclude it entirely from your DTI calculation. 
</p>
