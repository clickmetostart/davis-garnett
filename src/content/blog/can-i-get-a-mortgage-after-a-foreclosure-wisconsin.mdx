<h3>Can I get a mortgage after a foreclosure in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  A foreclosure is one of the hardest negative events to have on your credit — but it does not permanently bar you from homeownership. Here are the mandatory waiting periods by loan type:
</p>

<h3>Waiting Periods After Foreclosure</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>FHA Loans:</strong> 3 years from the foreclosure completion date (the date the property transferred out of your name)</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loans:</strong> 2 years from the foreclosure completion date</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loans:</strong> 3 years from the foreclosure completion date</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional (Fannie Mae):</strong> 7 years standard; reduced to 3 years with documented extenuating circumstances (job loss, medical event, divorce) and at least 10% down</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Counts as the Foreclosure Date?</h3>
<p>
  In Wisconsin, the clock starts from the <strong>sheriff's sale date</strong> or the date the deed transferred to the new owner — not when you stopped making payments. If there was a short sale instead, the waiting periods are shorter (typically 2–4 years depending on loan type and deficiency).
</p>

<h3>What to Do During the Waiting Period</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Open 2–3 secured credit cards and pay them off monthly</li>
  <li>Re-establish a positive payment history across all accounts</li>
  <li>Save aggressively — reserves and down payment funds send a strong signal</li>
  <li>Avoid any new collections or derogatory marks</li>
</ul>
