<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers a question we get constantly from first-generation homebuyers and families helping adult children buy their first home.</em></p>

<h3>Can I use gift money for a down payment in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  Yes — and it's extremely common. Many of my buyers in Madison get help from parents, grandparents, or other family members. Here's how it works by loan type:
</p>

<h3>FHA Loans</h3>
<p>
  <strong>100% of your down payment and closing costs can be a gift</strong> from a family member, fiancé, close friend, employer, or charitable organization. FHA is the most generous loan type for gift funds.
</p>

<h3>Conventional Loans</h3>
<p>
  Gift funds are allowed but with some restrictions. If you're putting less than 20% down, some of the funds must come from your own savings. With 20% or more down, the entire amount can be a gift. The donor must be a relative, domestic partner, or fiancé — not a stranger or business associate.
</p>

<h3>VA Loans</h3>
<p>
  Gift funds are fully allowed for VA loans, with no minimum borrower contribution required.
</p>

<h3>What Documentation Is Required?</h3>
<p>
  Every gift requires a <strong>gift letter</strong> signed by the donor that states:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>The dollar amount of the gift</li>
  <li>The donor's relationship to the buyer</li>
  <li>The property address</li>
  <li>A statement that the funds are a gift and do not need to be repaid</li>
</ul>
<p>
  We'll also need to source the gift — meaning we'll document where the money came from (typically 2 months of the donor's bank statements showing the funds).
</p>
