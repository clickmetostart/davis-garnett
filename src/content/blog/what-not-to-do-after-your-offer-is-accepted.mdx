<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Getting your offer accepted is exciting. You found a home, agreed on a price, signed the contract, and now you are looking forward to closing day. But an accepted offer is not the finish line. Your loan still has to go through steps like underwriting, appraisal, title work, insurance, and final checks. Many buyers do not realize they can create problems during this stage without meaning to.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The good news: most of these problems are easy to avoid when you know what not to do.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why This Time Period Matters</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your lender approved you based on your income, debts, and savings at the time you applied. If those things change before closing, the loan may need to be looked at again.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That can mean:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Extra paperwork</li>
<li>Delays in closing</li>
<li>In some cases, a change to your loan terms</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You do not need to be afraid of this. You just need to be careful and ask questions before you make big moves with money, credit, or your job.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Do Not Take On New Debt</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Taking on new debt is one of the fastest ways to cause trouble before closing.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">New debt can be:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>A new car loan</li>
<li>A new credit card</li>
<li>Store or furniture financing</li>
<li>A personal loan</li>
<li>A big jump in the balance on a card you already have</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even if the new payment seems small, it can still change your debt-to-income ratio. Your loan was approved using the payments you had at the time of application. If you add more, your lender may have to review the file again.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Buying furniture or appliances before closing may feel harmless, but the lender still has to count that new payment.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Simple rule: before you apply for new credit or make a big purchase on credit, <a href="https://madcityhomeloans.com/contact">talk to your mortgage team</a>.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Do Not Change Jobs Without Talking To Your Lender</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your income is a key part of your approval.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Changes like these can affect your loan:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Starting a new job</li>
<li>Moving from salary to commission or tips</li>
<li>Reducing your hours</li>
<li>Becoming self-employed</li>
<li>Changing the way you get paid</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even a change that seems positive may need extra proof. A higher bonus, more overtime, or new side income may need time and documentation before it can be counted.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You can sometimes change jobs during the process. But you should not do it without talking to your mortgage team first. The type of job, how you are paid, and the timing all matter.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Do Not Move Money Around Without a Paper Trail</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Moving money between accounts can also cause delays if it is not clear where the funds came from.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Examples include:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Large cash deposits</li>
<li>Transfers between bank accounts</li>
<li>Money sent through apps like Venmo or Zelle</li>
<li>Moving funds from savings or investments into checking</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your lender must show that your money came from acceptable sources, especially for your down payment, closing costs, and reserves.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A large deposit with no clear source is not always a deal breaker, but it will likely need to be explained and documented. That takes time.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Before you:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Accept a gift</li>
<li>Deposit cash</li>
<li>Move a large amount between accounts</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">ask your mortgage team what proof will be needed. It is much easier to set it up the right way from the start than to fix it later.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. Do Not Panic If the Appraisal Is Low</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The appraisal is the step where an appraiser gives an opinion of the home's value.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If the home appraises at or above the purchase price, you can usually move ahead as planned. If the appraisal comes in low, the buyer, seller, lender, and agents need to talk about options.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A low appraisal can change:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Your loan amount</li>
<li>How much cash you need to bring</li>
<li>Seller credits</li>
<li>The purchase price</li>
<li>The loan structure</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This does not always kill the deal. Often, people adjust.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For example:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>The buyer may bring some extra cash.</li>
<li>The seller may lower the price.</li>
<li>The agents may renegotiate the terms.</li>
<li>The lender may suggest a different loan setup.</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The key is not to panic. Stay calm, look at the numbers, and review choices with your team.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">5. Do Not Ignore Insurance and Title Details</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some delays have nothing to do with your income or credit. The home itself also has to be clear for the lender.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Two big pieces are:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Title:</strong> who owns the home and whether there are any liens, unpaid taxes, or legal issues</li>
<li><strong>Insurance:</strong> whether the property can be insured and what the cost will be</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Title problems can include old liens, unpaid taxes, or mistakes in past records. Insurance issues can show up if coverage is very expensive, hard to get, or does not meet the lender's rules.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These are not always in your control as a buyer, but you still play a part. Answer questions from the title company and insurance agent quickly and share any info they request. Good communication helps keep things on schedule.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">6. Do Not Delay When Your Lender Asks For Documents</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Once you are under contract, it is normal for underwriting to ask for more documents or updated versions of what you already gave.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They may ask for:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Recent pay stubs or bank statements</li>
<li>Explanations for deposits or withdrawals</li>
<li>Updated insurance information</li>
<li>Signed letters of explanation or forms</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This does not mean something is wrong. It usually means the underwriter is doing a careful review before giving final approval.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But if you wait too long to respond, it can delay your closing. A short delay in sending one document can turn into a bigger delay later in the process.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When your mortgage team asks for something, try to send it as soon as you can.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Simple Rule To Follow After Your Offer Is Accepted</h3>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Once your offer is accepted, follow this rule:</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Do not make big money or job changes without talking to your mortgage team first.</strong></p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That means:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Do not open new credit.</li>
<li>Do not finance furniture or appliances.</li>
<li>Do not buy a car.</li>
<li>Do not move large amounts of money around.</li>
<li>Do not change jobs or income without a conversation.</li>
<li>Do not make large unexplained deposits.</li>
<li>Do not ignore lender requests.</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most problems are easier to fix when they are caught early.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The mortgage process is not just about getting approved once. It is about staying approved all the way until you get the keys.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you are under contract or planning to buy soon, <a href="https://madcityhomeloans.com/contact">reach out to your mortgage team</a> before making any big financial moves. A quick question up front can save you from a stressful delay later.</p>
