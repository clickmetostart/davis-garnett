<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, walks first-time buyers through everything they need to know before making their first mortgage application.</em></p>

<h3>How do I get a mortgage as a first-time homebuyer in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  Buying your first home is the biggest financial decision of your life. Here's the process laid out simply, from someone who has guided hundreds of Wisconsin first-timers through it:
</p>

<h3>Step 1: Know Your Numbers Before You Start</h3>
<p>
  Pull your credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com. Know your score, and review for any errors. Check your monthly income (gross, not net) and your total monthly debt payments. These two numbers determine nearly everything about what you can borrow.
</p>

<h3>Step 2: Get Pre-Approved (Not Just Pre-Qualified)</h3>
<p>
  Don't start looking at homes without a real pre-approval. In Wisconsin's market, sellers won't take your offer seriously without it — and you need to know your real budget, not an estimate.
</p>

<h3>Step 3: Choose Your Loan Type</h3>
<p>The main options for first-timers in Wisconsin:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>FHA (3.5% down, 580+ score):</strong> Most forgiving, best for lower scores or limited savings</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional 3% (620+ score):</strong> Lower long-term cost if your score is strong</li>
  <li><strong>WHEDA Advantage:</strong> Below-market rate + down payment help for income-qualified buyers</li>
  <li><strong>VA (0% down, veterans only):</strong> Best deal available if you qualify</li>
  <li><strong>USDA (0% down, rural areas):</strong> Great for buyers in Waunakee, DeForest, Stoughton, etc.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Step 4: Don't Touch Your Credit or Finances After Application</h3>
<p>
  No new credit cards. No new car loans. No large unexplained deposits. No switching jobs. These are the moves that kill deals between pre-approval and closing.
</p>

<h3>Step 5: Show Up to Closing Ready</h3>
<p>
  Bring your ID and certified funds. Review your Closing Disclosure before the day of. Ask every question — that's what we're there for.
</p>
