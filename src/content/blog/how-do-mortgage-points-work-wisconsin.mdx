<h3>How do mortgage points work in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  A mortgage "point" is simply 1% of your loan amount paid upfront at closing in exchange for a lower interest rate. On a $300,000 loan, one point costs $3,000.
</p>

<h3>How Much Does a Point Reduce Your Rate?</h3>
<p>
  This varies by lender, market conditions, and loan type — but generally, one discount point reduces your rate by approximately <strong>0.25%</strong>. That translates to roughly $40–$50/month in savings on a $300,000 loan.
</p>

<h3>The Break-Even Math</h3>
<p>
  The key question is: how long will you keep the loan? If one point costs $3,000 and saves you $45/month, you break even in <strong>67 months</strong> (about 5.5 years). If you plan to stay and keep the loan longer than that, buying the point makes financial sense. If you'll sell or refinance sooner, don't buy them.
</p>

<h3>When Points Make Sense</h3>
<ul>
  <li>You're buying a forever home or long-term primary residence</li>
  <li>You have extra cash at closing beyond your down payment and reserves</li>
  <li>Rates are elevated and you want to lock in a lower payment permanently</li>
  <li>You've calculated the break-even and you'll clearly surpass it</li>
</ul>

<h3>When Points Don't Make Sense</h3>
<ul>
  <li>You're planning to sell or move within 3–5 years</li>
  <li>You need every dollar for reserves or home improvements after closing</li>
  <li>You expect rates to drop and plan to refinance within 2 years</li>
</ul>
