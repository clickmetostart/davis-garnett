<h3>How does a delayed financing exception work for real estate investors?</h3>

<p>
  The delayed financing exception is a Fannie Mae guideline that allows a buyer who purchases a property with cash to do a cash-out refinance <strong>immediately after closing</strong> — rather than waiting the typical 6-month seasoning period.
</p>

<h3>Why This Is a Powerful Strategy</h3>
<p>
  In competitive Wisconsin markets, cash offers win. With delayed financing, an investor can:
</p>
<ol>
  <li>Make a cash offer and beat out all financed competition</li>
  <li>Close quickly (often in 7–14 days)</li>
  <li>Immediately refinance and pull their cash back out</li>
  <li>Redeploy that capital to the next deal</li>
</ol>
<p>
  Essentially, you get all the competitive benefits of being a cash buyer — but you only need to have the cash tied up for 30–60 days.
</p>

<h3>Key Requirements</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The property must have been purchased entirely with cash (no existing financing)</li>
  <li>The refinance loan amount cannot exceed the original purchase price plus documented closing costs and improvement costs</li>
  <li>Must be a conventional conforming loan (not FHA, VA, or USDA)</li>
  <li>Title must show the borrower owned the property free and clear before the refi</li>
  <li>A full appraisal is required</li>
</ul>

<h3>For Realtors: A Conversation Worth Having</h3>
<p>
  If you work with investors who have capital but want to preserve liquidity, this strategy is worth bringing up. It's how the sophisticated investors are competing in Madison's tight inventory market right now.
</p>
