<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers a question we hear most in hot Wisconsin markets where buyers waive contingencies.</em></p>

<h3>What is a home inspection and do I need one in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  A home inspection is an independent, visual assessment of a property's condition — roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and more — performed by a licensed inspector before you finalize your purchase.
</p>

<h3>Is It Required for Your Loan?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans:</strong> No formal home inspection is required by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. The appraisal checks value and basic safety, not comprehensive condition.</li>
  <li><strong>FHA Loans:</strong> FHA appraisals are more detailed than conventional ones and must confirm the property meets HUD Minimum Property Standards — but this is still not a full home inspection.</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loans:</strong> VA requires an <em>appraisal</em> that includes minimum property requirements (MPRs), but again, not a full third-party inspection.</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loans:</strong> Same as FHA — the appraisal checks basic habitability but a separate inspection is not required by the loan itself.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Should You Get One Anyway?</h3>
<p>
  <strong>Yes. Always.</strong> I've seen buyers waive inspections to win offers in competitive markets and later discover foundation issues, failing HVAC systems, or knob-and-tube wiring that costs $15,000–$40,000 to fix. A $500 inspection is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy on a $400,000 asset.
</p>
<p>
  If you're in a multiple-offer situation, consider negotiating an "information only" inspection — you get the inspection but agree not to ask for repairs. You still learn what you're buying.
</p>
