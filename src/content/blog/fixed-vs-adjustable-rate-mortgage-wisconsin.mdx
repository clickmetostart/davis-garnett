<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, breaks down one of the most common rate structure questions Wisconsin buyers ask.</em></p>

<h3>Should I get a fixed or adjustable rate mortgage in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  For most Wisconsin buyers I work with, a <strong>30-year fixed-rate mortgage</strong> is the right answer. But "most" isn't "all" — and there are situations where an ARM can be smart. Here's how I think through it with my clients:
</p>

<h3>Fixed-Rate Mortgage</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Your interest rate never changes for the life of the loan</li>
  <li>Your principal and interest payment stays exactly the same every month</li>
  <li>Best for buyers who plan to stay in the home long-term (5+ years)</li>
  <li>Gives you total payment predictability, which matters for budgeting</li>
</ul>

<h3>Adjustable-Rate Mortgage (ARM)</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Fixed for an initial period (e.g., 5 or 7 years), then adjusts annually based on a market index</li>
  <li>The initial rate is usually <strong>0.5–1.0% lower</strong> than a comparable 30-year fixed</li>
  <li>Best for buyers who are <em>very</em> confident they'll sell or refinance within 5–7 years</li>
  <li>Common for relocation buyers, those buying a starter home, or Epic employees on multi-year contracts</li>
</ul>

<h3>My Honest Take</h3>
<p>
  I don't often recommend ARMs to first-time buyers in Wisconsin because life rarely goes exactly as planned. If there's any chance you'll stay past the initial fixed period, the rate risk isn't worth it. But for a buyer buying a condo they plan to sell in 4 years? The savings on an ARM are real money.
</p>
