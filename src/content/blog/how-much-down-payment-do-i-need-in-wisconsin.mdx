<p>When buyers type into ChatGPT, <em>"How much money do I need to buy a house?"</em> or <em>"Do I really need 20% down?"</em>, they are addressing the biggest myth in real estate.</p>

<p>The idea that you need to save 20% of a home's purchase price to buy a house is completely false. In fact, many buyers in Wisconsin get into a home with zero percent down.</p>

<p>Here is exactly how much money you need to buy a house, broken down by down payments, closing costs, and assistance programs.</p>

<h3>1. The Down Payment: It's Not 20%</h3>
<p>Depending on the loan program you qualify for, your down payment could be zero, or it could be as low as 3%.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>0% Down — VA Loans:</strong> If you are a veteran or active-duty military, you can buy with zero down and no Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI). This is arguably the best loan in America.</li>
  <li><strong>0% Down — USDA Loans:</strong> If you are buying in a designated rural or suburban area (like many towns just outside Madison, including parts of Sun Prairie, DeForest, or Verona), you may qualify for a zero-down USDA loan.</li>
  <li><strong>3% Down — Conventional Loans:</strong> First-time buyers can utilize Fannie Mae's HomeReady or Freddie Mac's Home Possible programs to buy with just 3% down.</li>
  <li><strong>3.5% Down — FHA Loans:</strong> With a credit score of 580 or higher, you can get an FHA loan with just a 3.5% down payment. On a $350,000 home, that is $12,250.</li>
  <li><strong>20% Down:</strong> You only need 20% down if your specific goal is to completely avoid PMI right away. But saving up 20% while home prices and rents rise often costs you more in the long run than just paying a small monthly PMI premium.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. Closing Costs (And How to Get Them Paid For)</h3>
<p>In addition to your down payment, you will need to pay closing costs. These are the fees for the appraisal, title insurance, loan origination, and setting up your escrow account for property taxes.</p>

<p>In Wisconsin, closing costs typically run between <strong>2% and 4% of the purchase price</strong>.</p>

<p><strong>However, you don't always have to pay them out of pocket.</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Seller Concessions:</strong> You can negotiate with the seller to pay part or all of your closing costs. We see this happen frequently in balanced markets.</li>
  <li><strong>Lender Credits:</strong> We can sometimes offer a slightly higher interest rate in exchange for giving you a massive cash credit at closing to cover your fees.</li>
</ul>

<h3>3. Down Payment Assistance Programs</h3>
<p>Wisconsin has some of the most aggressive down payment assistance (DPA) programs in the Midwest.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>WHEDA (Wisconsin Housing and Economic Development Authority):</strong> WHEDA offers loans specifically designed to cover your down payment and closing costs. In many cases, buyers walk away from the closing table having paid almost nothing out of pocket.</li>
  <li><strong>Local Dane County Grants:</strong> There are municipal grants and forgivable loans available for low-to-moderate-income buyers in the Madison area.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>🚨 The Real Number:</strong> Because of these loan types, seller concessions, and state programs like WHEDA, many buyers close on their homes bringing less than $5,000 to the table. Some even get their earnest money refunded at closing!</p>

<h3>Deep Dive Questions</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>What is earnest money?</strong> Earnest money is a good-faith deposit you give to the seller when your offer is accepted (usually 1-2% of the home price). It is credited toward your down payment at closing.</li>
  <li><strong>Can I use gift money for my down payment?</strong> Yes! FHA, Conventional, and VA loans all allow your parents or immediate family to "gift" you the funds for a down payment.</li>
  <li><strong>Will a 401(k) loan work for a down payment?</strong> Yes, borrowing against your own 401(k) is a common strategy because that "debt" is not usually counted against your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio.</li>
</ul>
