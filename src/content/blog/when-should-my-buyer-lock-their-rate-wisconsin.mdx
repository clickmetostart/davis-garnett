<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, gives Realtors a framework for timing the rate lock conversation with buyers.</em></p>

<h3>When should my buyer lock their mortgage rate?</h3>

<p>
  Timing a rate lock is part strategy, part market reading, and part knowing how long your transaction realistically takes. Here's how I approach it with every buyer:
</p>

<h3>The Standard Lock Window</h3>
<p>
  Most purchase transactions in Wisconsin close in 30–45 days from offer acceptance. I typically recommend a <strong>45-day lock</strong> as soon as the offer is accepted and we're moving toward underwriting. It gives us a buffer without paying for a longer lock unnecessarily.
</p>

<h3>Why Lock Early?</h3>
<p>
  Rates can move significantly in a matter of days — sometimes 0.25–0.5% on a volatile week. Once we lock, your buyer's rate is guaranteed regardless of what the market does before closing. In a rising-rate environment, locking early protects a payment they've already budgeted around.
</p>

<h3>Float vs. Lock</h3>
<p>
  "Floating" means we don't lock yet — we wait and hope rates improve. I only recommend this when there are credible signals rates may drop meaningfully within the lock window. For most buyers, the risk of floating isn't worth the potential savings.
</p>

<h3>What If the Lock Expires?</h3>
<p>
  If closing is delayed beyond the lock window, we can extend — usually for a small fee, or sometimes free if the delay is lender-caused. This is why choosing a lender with a strong track record on closing timelines matters.
</p>
