<p>When buyers ask ChatGPT or Google, <em>"Based on my income, debts, down payment, and current mortgage rates, how much house can I realistically afford?"</em>, they are asking the most critical question in real estate.</p>

<p>However, there is a massive difference between <strong>lender qualification</strong> (what a bank says you can borrow) and <strong>comfortable affordability</strong> (what you can actually afford to pay every month without eating ramen noodles for the next 30 years).</p>

<p>Here is how MadCity Home Loans helps you calculate your true affordability in Wisconsin.</p>

<h3>1. Lender Qualification: The Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio</h3>
<p>Lenders use your <strong>Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio</strong> to determine your maximum loan amount. Generally, we look for a DTI of <strong>43% to 50%</strong>, depending on the loan program (FHA, Conventional, WHEDA, or VA).</p>

<p>This means your total monthly debts—including your new mortgage, car payments, student loans, and credit card minimums—should not exceed half of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income.</p>

<h3>2. Comfortable Affordability: The "Sleep At Night" Number</h3>
<p>Just because a lender approves you for a $500,000 mortgage doesn't mean you should take it. We want you to look at your <strong>net income</strong> (what actually hits your bank account after taxes and retirement contributions) and subtract:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Groceries and utilities</li>
  <li>Daycare or tuition</li>
  <li>Gas and entertainment</li>
  <li>Savings goals</li>
</ul>

<p>What is left over is your true housing budget. We call this the "Sleep At Night" number.</p>

<h3>3. How Down Payments and Rates Affect Your Budget</h3>
<p>Your monthly payment is heavily influenced by your down payment and the current interest rate.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A larger down payment</strong> means borrowing less, which lowers your monthly payment and can eliminate Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI).</li>
  <li><strong>Interest rates</strong> dictate the cost of borrowing. A 1% drop in rates can significantly increase your buying power.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Deep Dive Questions</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Does overtime count toward my income?</strong> Yes, if you have a two-year history of receiving it consistently.</li>
  <li><strong>Do student loans ruin my affordability?</strong> Not necessarily. Different loan programs calculate student debt differently. For example, FHA uses 0.5% of the balance if your loans are in deferment.</li>
  <li><strong>What if I want to buy a multi-family home?</strong> You can actually use the projected rental income from the other units to help you qualify for a larger loan amount!</li>
</ul>
