<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, settles the confusion between these two terms — because in today's market, it really matters.</em></p>

<h3>What is the difference between mortgage pre-qualification and pre-approval?</h3>

<p>
  These two terms get used interchangeably, but they are fundamentally different — and in a competitive Wisconsin market, using the wrong one can cost you a home.
</p>

<h3>Pre-Qualification</h3>
<p>
  A pre-qualification is a quick, informal estimate based on <strong>self-reported information</strong>. You tell a lender your income, debts, and assets. They run a quick calculation (sometimes with a soft credit pull, sometimes not) and issue a letter. No documents are verified. No credit is pulled in depth. 
</p>
<p>
  A pre-qual letter tells a seller almost nothing. Listing agents know this.
</p>

<h3>Pre-Approval</h3>
<p>
  A pre-approval is a real underwriting review. We pull your credit, collect and verify your income documents (W-2s, pay stubs, tax returns), and review your assets. The file goes through our system and often through an actual automated underwriting engine (DU or LP). The result is a conditional approval — a real commitment that this buyer qualifies.
</p>

<h3>Underwritten Pre-Approval: The Gold Standard</h3>
<p>
  At MadCity Home Loans, we take it one step further with an underwritten pre-approval — where an actual human underwriter reviews your file before you make an offer. When listing agents see this, they know the deal is as close to guaranteed as possible.
</p>
<p>
  In a multiple-offer situation, this can be the deciding factor.
</p>
