<h2>What Makes Investment Property Lending Different</h2>

<p>Financing an investment property is fundamentally different from financing a primary residence, and lenders treat it that way. The key differences:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Down payment requirements:</strong> Investment properties typically require a minimum of 15–25% down, depending on the loan program and number of units. Zero-down and low-down products are not available for investment purchases.</li>
  <li><strong>Rate adjustments:</strong> Investment property loans carry rate add-ons (called loan-level price adjustments, or LLPAs) above primary residence rates. This is standard across the industry.</li>
  <li><strong>Income documentation:</strong> For conventional investment loans, lenders evaluate both the borrower's personal income and, in some cases, the projected rental income from the property.</li>
  <li><strong>Reserve requirements:</strong> Most programs require the borrower to demonstrate cash reserves — often covering several months of payments on both the primary residence and the investment property.</li>
</ul>

<p>Understanding these distinctions upfront helps investors enter the market with realistic expectations and a financing strategy that actually works.</p>

<h2>DSCR Loans: The Game-Changer for Madison Investors</h2>

<p>Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) loans have become one of the most important tools in the real estate investor's financing toolkit. While MadCity Home Loans does not directly offer DSCR loans, we believe understanding how they work is essential for any investor exploring their options in the market.</p>

<h3>How DSCR Loans Work</h3>

<p>Instead of qualifying based on the borrower's personal income and W-2s, a DSCR loan qualifies based on the property's income relative to its debt obligation. The DSCR is calculated as:</p>

<p><strong>DSCR = Monthly Rental Income ÷ Monthly Debt Service (PITIA)</strong></p>

<p>A DSCR of 1.0 means the property's rent exactly covers the mortgage payment. Most DSCR lenders want to see a ratio of 1.0 to 1.25 or higher, though some programs offer "DSCR below 1.0" options for investors with strong credit and assets.</p>

<h3>Why DSCR Works in Madison</h3>

<p>Madison's strong rental market is one of its greatest assets for DSCR qualification. With median monthly rents for single-family homes often well above $2,000 in Dane County — and higher in desirable neighborhoods — properties frequently achieve the income ratios required for DSCR qualification. A 1031 exchange investor, a self-employed buyer with complicated taxes, or a high-net-worth individual who doesn't want to subject their personal financial picture to traditional underwriting scrutiny can often qualify for a DSCR loan far more smoothly than a conventional investment product.</p>

<h3>Who DSCR Loans Are Built For</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Self-employed investors</strong> whose tax returns understate their true income</li>
  <li><strong>High-volume investors</strong> with large existing real estate portfolios where debt-to-income ratios create conventional qualification problems</li>
  <li><strong>Short-term rental operators</strong> — DSCR programs are increasingly accommodating Airbnb and VRBO income documentation in markets like Madison</li>
  <li><strong>Out-of-state investors</strong> targeting Madison without a local income footprint</li>
  <li><strong>Investors who want speed</strong> — DSCR underwriting is typically faster than full-documentation conventional programs because the focus is on the asset, not a deep personal financial dive</li>
</ul>

<h2>Conventional Investment Property Loans in Madison</h2>

<p>For investors with strong personal income and W-2 or self-employment documentation that holds up well under traditional underwriting, conventional investment loans remain a solid option — particularly for buyers who want the lowest possible long-term rate and plan to hold properties for years.</p>

<p>Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac allow financing on investment properties up to four units under conventional guidelines. Key thresholds:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>1–2 unit investment properties:</strong> Typically 15–20% minimum down</li>
  <li><strong>3–4 unit investment properties:</strong> Typically 20–25% minimum down</li>
  <li><strong>Credit:</strong> Most conventional investment programs prefer 680+ (higher credit thresholds access better pricing)</li>
  <li><strong>Reserves:</strong> Two to six months of PITIA reserves is common — more for borrowers with multiple financed properties</li>
</ul>

<p>For investors with more than 10 financed properties, Fannie Mae's guidelines place limits on conventional financing, which is often where DSCR and non-QM products step in.</p>

<h2>Multi-Family Financing: Duplexes, Triplexes, and Four-Plexes in Madison</h2>

<p>Owner-occupied multi-family purchases — where the buyer lives in one unit and rents the others — are one of the most powerful wealth-building strategies available to Madison-area buyers. They're also unique in how they're financed.</p>

<p>When a buyer purchases a 2–4 unit property as their primary residence:
- <strong>FHA financing is available with 3.5% down</strong>
- <strong>Conventional financing starts at 5% down</strong>
- <strong>VA financing allows zero-down purchase of up to four units</strong> for qualifying veterans who occupy one unit</p>

<p>In Madison's market, a duplex or triplex purchase where rental income offsets most or all of the mortgage payment is a legitimate path to homeownership with a built-in investment component. <h2>Using Home Equity to Fund Your First Investment Property</h2>

<p>A common path for first-time Madison investors is using equity from their primary residence — through a <a href="/knowledge-hub/home-equity-loan-heloc-madison-wi">home equity loan</a>, HELOC, or cash-out <a href="/knowledge-hub/refinancing-guide-rob-miller-provisor">refinance</a> — to fund the down payment on an investment property. This approach avoids liquidating retirement accounts or savings and puts appreciating real estate equity to work generating rental income.</p>

<p>If this is your strategy, the sequencing matters: pulling equity from the primary home first, then applying for the investment property loan, keeps the debt-to-income calculations cleaner and avoids complications during investment property underwriting.</p>
