<h3>Can my buyer use seller credits to cover closing costs?</h3>

<p>
  Yes — and this is one of the most powerful tools available for buyers who are cash-light but income-strong. Here are the limits by loan type that every Wisconsin Realtor needs to know:
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>FHA Loans:</strong> Sellers can contribute up to <strong>6%</strong> of the purchase price toward the buyer's closing costs.</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans (less than 10% down):</strong> Seller concessions are capped at <strong>3%</strong> of the purchase price.</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans (10–25% down):</strong> Cap rises to <strong>6%</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans (25%+ down):</strong> Cap is <strong>9%</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loans:</strong> Sellers can pay all of the buyer's loan-related closing costs, plus up to <strong>4%</strong> in additional concessions (paying off debts, buying down the rate, etc.).</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loans:</strong> Seller concessions capped at <strong>6%</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Seller Credits Can and Can't Pay For</h3>
<p>
  Seller credits can cover lender fees, title insurance, prepaid taxes and insurance, and discount points — but they <strong>cannot</strong> cover the buyer's down payment. They reduce out-of-pocket cash at closing, not the required equity contribution.
</p>

<h3>Strategic Tip for Listing Agents</h3>
<p>
  When negotiating, consider framing seller credits as part of a higher offer price. A buyer who offers $355,000 with $5,000 in credits is often better for a seller than a $350,000 clean offer, depending on the seller's net position and the appraisal situation.
</p>
