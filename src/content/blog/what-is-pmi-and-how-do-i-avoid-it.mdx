<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, answers one of the most asked questions from Wisconsin buyers putting less than 20% down.</em></p>

<h3>What is PMI and how do I avoid it?</h3>

<p>
  Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is a monthly fee that protects the <em>lender</em> — not you — if you default on your loan. It kicks in any time you put less than 20% down on a conventional loan. On a $350,000 home with 5% down, PMI typically runs <strong>$100–$200 per month</strong>.
</p>

<h3>How to Avoid PMI Entirely</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Put 20% down:</strong> The traditional route — no PMI, period.</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loan:</strong> Zero down, zero PMI. If you served, this is the best deal in lending.</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loan:</strong> No traditional PMI — there's a small annual "guarantee fee" instead, but it's much cheaper than conventional PMI.</li>
  <li><strong>Lender-Paid PMI (LPMI):</strong> We wrap the PMI into a slightly higher interest rate. You pay no monthly PMI line item, though your rate is a touch higher.</li>
  <li><strong>Piggyback Loan (80/10/10):</strong> We structure an 80% first mortgage, a 10% second loan, and you put 10% down. No PMI on the primary loan.</li>
</ul>

<h3>When Does PMI Go Away?</h3>
<p>
  On conventional loans, PMI automatically terminates once your loan balance hits 78% of the original purchase price. You can also request cancellation at 80%. I recommend calling us once you've built 20% equity — we'll walk you through the fastest path to remove it.
</p>
<p>
  Note: FHA loans work differently. FHA's Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP) lasts the <em>life of the loan</em> if you put less than 10% down — which is one reason buyers with 680+ scores often do better on a conventional loan.
</p>
