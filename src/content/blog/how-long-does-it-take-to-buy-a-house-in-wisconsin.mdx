<p><em>Rob Miller, Branch Manager at MadCity Home Loans, gives Wisconsin buyers a realistic look at the full timeline from start to keys.</em></p>

<h3>How long does it take to buy a house in Wisconsin?</h3>

<p>
  From the day you start the process to the day you close, the typical Wisconsin home purchase takes <strong>60–90 days</strong>. Here's how that breaks down:
</p>

<h3>Phase 1: Pre-Approval (1–3 Days)</h3>
<p>
  If you have your documents ready, we can issue a pre-approval letter in 24–48 hours. A full underwritten pre-approval takes 3–5 business days. This is the step most buyers rush — don't. A stronger pre-approval sets up everything that follows.
</p>

<h3>Phase 2: Home Search (2–8 Weeks)</h3>
<p>
  Highly variable. In a hot market like Madison, buyers often find a home within 2–4 weeks of searching. In slower markets or niche price points, it can stretch to 2+ months. This phase is entirely controlled by the market and your Realtor.
</p>

<h3>Phase 3: Offer to Accepted Contract (1–7 Days)</h3>
<p>
  Writing the offer, negotiating, and executing a final purchase contract typically takes 1–5 business days, sometimes faster in competitive situations.
</p>

<h3>Phase 4: Under Contract to Close (21–45 Days)</h3>
<p>
  This is the mortgage pipeline phase. Here's what happens:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>Days 1–5: Loan application, initial disclosures, appraisal ordered</li>
  <li>Days 5–15: Appraisal completed, underwriting review</li>
  <li>Days 15–25: Conditions cleared, final underwriting approval</li>
  <li>Days 25–30: Clear to close, Closing Disclosure issued, 3-day waiting period</li>
  <li>Day 30–45: Closing — keys in hand</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Causes Delays?</h3>
<p>
  Missing documents, slow appraisals, title issues, and lender backlogs are the top culprits. Choosing a responsive local lender (versus an online bank) dramatically reduces delay risk.
</p>
