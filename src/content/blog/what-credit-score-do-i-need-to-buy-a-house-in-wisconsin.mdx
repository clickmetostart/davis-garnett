<p><em>"What credit score do I need to get a mortgage?"</em></p>

<p>This is one of the most frequently searched real estate questions on Google and ChatGPT. Buyers constantly ask variations of this, such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li><em>"Can I get a mortgage with a 600 credit score?"</em></li>
  <li><em>"What's the minimum credit score to buy a house?"</em></li>
  <li><em>"Can I get a mortgage with bad credit?"</em></li>
</ul>

<p>The answer is much more flexible than most people think. Here is the straight truth, broken down by loan type.</p>

<h3>1. The Minimum Score Requirements</h3>
<p>Different loan programs have entirely different credit requirements.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>FHA Loans: 580 Minimum.</strong> With a 580 score, you only need 3.5% down. If your score is between 500 and 579, you can technically still qualify for an FHA loan, but you will be required to put 10% down. (At MadCity, we work with scores down to 580 regularly).</li>
  <li><strong>VA Loans: 580–620 Minimum.</strong> The VA itself does not actually set a minimum credit score, but individual lenders do. Most lenders (including MadCity Home Loans) look for a 580 to 620 minimum.</li>
  <li><strong>Conventional Loans: 620 Minimum.</strong> To get through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac's automated underwriting systems, you need a 620. However, to get the <em>best</em> interest rate tiers on a Conventional loan, you typically want a score of 740 or higher.</li>
  <li><strong>USDA Loans: 640 Minimum.</strong> USDA loans usually require a 640 for automated approval. If you fall slightly below this, you may still qualify through manual underwriting.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>💡 Can I get a mortgage with a 600 credit score?</strong><br />
Absolutely. With a 600 credit score, you comfortably clear the minimum requirement for an FHA loan (580) and many VA loans.</p>

<h3>2. Can I get a mortgage with "bad" credit?</h3>
<p>Yes, but you have to be strategic. A lower credit score simply means the lender is taking on more risk. To offset that risk, you might have to pay a higher interest rate or put down a larger down payment.</p>

<p>However, the "full picture" matters. We have closed loans for borrowers with mid-600 scores who had excellent income histories, extremely low debt-to-income (DTI) ratios, and solid cash reserves. Your credit score is just one piece of the puzzle.</p>

<h3>3. How can I improve my credit before buying a house?</h3>
<p>If your score isn't quite there yet, do not wait and guess—call us. We have helped hundreds of buyers get from a 560 to a 620 in just 60 to 90 days.</p>

<p>Here are the fastest ways to boost your score:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pay down revolving balances:</strong> Getting your credit card balances below 30% of their total limit is the fastest way to jump your score.</li>
  <li><strong>Don't close old accounts:</strong> Closing old credit cards shortens your credit history length, which can actually drop your score.</li>
  <li><strong>Will paying off collections help?</strong> Not always! Sometimes, paying off a very old collection account "reactivates" the negative activity date on your credit report, dropping your score temporarily. <strong>Always speak to a mortgage professional before paying off old debt.</strong></li>
</ul>
