<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You’ve found the home you love. The list price? $400,000. You’re ready to make an offer, and naturally, you’re wondering if you should come in a little under asking   maybe $390K  to save some cash.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It feels like the smart move, right?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But what if I told you there’s a strategy that could save you way more than just $10K off the purchase price   not just once, but every single month?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yep. Let’s talk about the $10K trick most buyers don’t even know exists.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>The Smarter Play: Offer Full Price, Ask for a Concession</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead of offering $390K on that $400K home, what if you offered full price   but negotiated a $10,000 seller concession?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Here’s what that could look like:</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You offer the full $400,000…<br>But ask the seller to give you back $10,000 to cover mortgage points.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>What are mortgage points?</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mortgage points (aka discount points) are upfront fees you pay at closing to reduce your interest rate. One point typically costs 1% of your loan amount and knocks off about 0.25% from your rate.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So let’s break it down with real numbers.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Real Life Example: Why This Trick Works</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let’s say you’re putting 5% down on a $400,000 home.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That gives you a loan amount of $380,000.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Now imagine this:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>💰 You spend $10,000 on points (about 2.5 points)</li>



<li>📉 Your interest rate drops from <strong>7% ➝ 6.38%</strong></li>



<li>💸 Your monthly mortgage payment goes from<strong> $2,661 ➝ $2,497</strong></li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s<strong> $164/month</strong> saved   every month   for as long as you have the loan.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Over just 5 years, that adds up to<strong>&nbsp;$9,840</strong>. Over 10 years?&nbsp;<strong>Nearly $20,000</strong>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So instead of saving $10K upfront by offering a lower price, you’re creating thousands more in long-term savings   simply by shifting how the money is used.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>But Why Would a Seller Agree?</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here’s the thing: a seller is usually more focused on the sales price than the net proceeds   especially if they want to keep the comps strong in the neighborhood.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">An offer at $400K with a $10K concession can be more appealing to a seller than an offer at $390K   because it still shows as a $400K sale on paper.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And for you? That $10K turns into serious savings over time.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Win-win.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>When This Trick Works Best</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This strategy isn’t one-size-fits-all, but it’s golden in a few key scenarios:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">✅ You’re buying in a balanced or buyer-friendly market<br>✅ The home’s been sitting for a few weeks and the seller is motivated<br>✅ You want a lower monthly payment without waiting for rates to drop<br>✅ You’ve got a little wiggle room in your loan approval for concessions</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s also perfect if you’re planning to stay in the home for several years and want to maximize long-term savings.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You can Google a “mortgage points calculator” and plug in your scenario to see what the potential savings look like.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Or… you can reach out to your mortgage pro (👋 that’s me!) and we’ll do it together.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I’ll help you compare both scenarios side by side   and show you if this strategy makes sense for your specific situation.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Final Thought</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Home buying is all about the strategy. And sometimes the best financial move isn’t the most obvious one.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So before you try to save a few thousand off the purchase price, ask yourself:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">👉 Could I make my money work harder by using it to buy down my rate instead?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because while $10K might sound like a small shift... that $164/month in savings could be the difference between stress and stability.</p>
